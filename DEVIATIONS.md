### DEV-001: electrode RMS lower bound lowered from 5 uV to 1 uV
- Date: 2026-09-29
- Preregistration section(s): §5.1 (and §20)
- Item and its tag (LOCKED / PLACEHOLDER): electrode RMS lower bound (untagged "~5 uV" in the document, treated as locked)
- Before: 5 uV
- After: 1 uV (config.yml preprocessing.bad_channel.rms_min_uv). rms_max_uv (150), the flat-line rule and the saturation rule are unchanged.
- Why: the rule acted as an amplitude floor, not an electrode-quality check. Sub-008 t1: 55 of 64 channels below 5 uV, median channel 3.90 uV, the four flagged electrodes at 88 to 122% of that median with normal alpha. Sub-086 t1: 19 of 64 channels below 5 uV. The rule flagged 2 of 12 pilot subjects (16.7%) versus the 1-2% expected in §4.2, and did not identify faulty electrodes.
- Real data loaded at the time? yes: pilot subjects only, all on the training side; no test-subject data read; no full-cohort result seen.
- Could this affect G0 or a C1–C4 verdict? How? It changes which recordings survive the bad-electrode exclusion (§12) and sets the lower RMS bound that §5.1 also uses for 1-s segments in B5. The paper's Limitations must report it.
- Approved by: Daniel Voldman
- Commit: 3fc4e4f

### DEV-002: excessive_blink_correction exclusion rule
- Date: 2026-09-29
- Preregistration section(s): §12 and §5.1
- Item and its tag (LOCKED / PLACEHOLDER): per-recording exclusion rules (§12); the new rule is not in the document
- Before: no such rule. §12 lists the units check, the bad electrode, the minimum clean data and divergence.
- After: a new per-recording exclusion rule "excessive_blink_correction": a recording is excluded if either channel's blink-corrected time exceeds 10% of the trimmed recording. It is placed after the bad-electrode rule and before the clean-data minimum.
- Why: blink correction runs before the 150 uV RMS rule, so a large slow artifact could be clipped and pass as clean (see IMP-013).
- Real data loaded at the time? yes: pilot subjects only, all on the training side; no test-subject data read.
- Could this affect G0 or a C1–C4 verdict? How? None of the 16 pilot recordings is excluded by it (largest corrected fraction 0.0474), but it can change which recordings survive. The paper's Limitations must report it.
- Approved by: Daniel Voldman
- Commit: 67a08e8

### DEV-003: start-up exemption of the state-beyond-10-SD divergence flag
- Date: 2026-09-29
- Preregistration section(s): §7.5 (divergence, defined); §10.2 (0.5 s burn-in after initialization)
- Item and its tag (LOCKED / PLACEHOLDER): the divergence definition of §7.5 (LOCKED design). The 10x multiple and the SD scale are unchanged. The exemption length is a new placeholder.
- Before: a segment is diverged if it contains a NaN/Inf, a covariance that is not positive definite after adding 1e-9 I, or any neural state beyond 10x its steady-state SD, checked at every step from the first.
- After: the state-beyond-10-SD flag, and only that flag, is not raised for the first N observation samples after each (re)initialization, N = round(startup_exempt_s x 256) = 128 (0.5 s). The NaN/Inf, covariance-not-positive-definite and linalg checks stay active from step 1. The multiple (10), the SD scale (the stored neural_variance) and the divergence rule for all later steps are unchanged. Config: ukf.divergence.startup_exempt_s = 0.5 (placeholder; §10.2 already discards the first 0.5 s after initialization as burn-in). The monitor reports startup_exempt_steps.
- Why: the filter starts at the prior steady state and the parameter estimates start at their priors, so on data whose true parameters differ from the prior the states move to the data before the parameters follow, and the rule flags a healthy start-up (synthetic series 18: p = (182, 289), g12 = 12, g21 = 27 was flagged at step 2 although its true trajectory never exceeds 8.8 SD from its own fixed point).
- Real data loaded at the time? Pilot subjects had been loaded for B3 to B6 (DEV-001, DEV-002); this change was designed and measured on synthetic data only; no test-subject data.
- Measured effect (20 healthy synthetic series, 8 s each, p ~ U[150, 300] per node, gains from {0, 5, 12, 27}, seeds 500 to 519; reference at the current posterior-mean parameters, IMP-029): flagged 3 of 20 without the exemption (series 15, 18, 19), 2 of 20 with it (series 15 and 19). Series 18 ran all 2048 steps with the exemption. All 17 unflagged series ran to completion with no fixed-point fallback.
- OPEN RISK (recorded, not a change): the state-SD rule flags strong two-way coupling. Series 15 (g12 = g21 = 27, p = (249, 164)) and 19 (g12 = g21 = 27, p = (175, 152)) are flagged at steps 375 and 134 in the filter, and their TRUE trajectories are 13.8 and 36.1 SD from their own true fixed points (4.5 and 22 percent of samples beyond 10 SD), so no reference point can fix it: the SD scale comes from the uncoupled 600-s noise-driven simulation (IMP-017) and is too small for those regimes. No regime is exempted and neither the multiple nor the SD scale is changed. To be decided at G0 on pilot/synthetic data (whether the top coupling level triggers the rule in the gate series, and what that means for §12 and G0).
- Could this affect G0 or a C1-C4 verdict? How? It reduces divergence flags in the first 0.5 s of every filter run; a filter that blows up in the first 0.5 s is still caught by the other three checks and by the rule from step 129 on. The open risk can make strongly coupled G0 series count as diverged.
- Approved by: Daniel Voldman
- Commit: 06d84da

### DEV-004: looser tolerance for SMOOTHED COVARIANCES in the Numba validation
- Date: 2026-09-30
- Preregistration section(s): §7.5 (performance path: Numba filtered and smoothed means and covariances compared with filterpy's at rtol = 1e-8, atol = 1e-10, PLACEHOLDER); §16.5.2
- Item and its tag (LOCKED / PLACEHOLDER): the validation tolerance of the Numba port, PLACEHOLDER. §7.5 gives one tolerance for "filtered and smoothed means and covariances" and no carve-out for the smoother covariance.
- Before: rtol 1e-8, atol 1e-10 for every filtered and smoothed mean and covariance (config ukf.numba.rtol, ukf.numba.atol).
- After: SMOOTHED COVARIANCES ONLY use rtol 1e-6, atol 1e-8 (config ukf.numba.rtol_smoothed_cov, atol_smoothed_cov, placeholder, fixed before the rerun). Everything else keeps rtol 1e-8, atol 1e-10: filtered means and covariances, smoothed means, S, NIS, the pass-1 recording-level parameters and carries, the pass-2 window trajectories and delayed S, and tuning.recording_nis. The downstream statistics are the acceptance check and their tests stay strict.
- Why: the unscented RTS smoother inverts the predicted covariance Pb, whose condition number is about 1e8 (median 1.2e8, maximum 5.4e8 over the steps of the 4-s coupled test series). Last-bit differences between two correct implementations (LAPACK potrf against the explicit Cholesky loop, LU inverse against closed form, two BLAS builds) therefore reach about 1e-8 in smoothed covariances. Evidence:
  1. Per-scenario maximum absolute error, number of elements above the strict tolerance, and worst error as a multiple of the strict tolerance (filtered x / filtered P / smoothed x / smoothed P): coupled asymmetric 19-D 1.8e-12 / 1.1e-11 / 2.1e-10 / 8.1e-9, 170 of 369,664, 27.5; M1 17-D 1.4e-12 / 8.6e-12 / 2.3e-10 / 8.8e-9, 2 of 147,968, 4.9; fix_EI 17-D 1.4e-12 / 5.6e-12 / 9.1e-11 / 3.5e-9, 8 of 147,968, 2.3; tie_p 18-D 1.5e-12 / 8.1e-12 / 1.6e-10 / 1.5e-8, 2 of 165,888, 4.7; tie_g 18-D 3.0e-12 / 8.6e-12 / 2.3e-10 / 7.0e-9, 0, 0.62; all three 15-D 1.6e-12 / 5.2e-12 / 5.7e-11 / 2.3e-9, 0, 0.72; M1 + fix_EI 15-D 2.4e-12 / 7.5e-12 / 5.7e-11 / 1.6e-9, 2 of 115,200, 1.8; fixed-parameter window 12-D 9.7e-13 / 5.2e-12 / 4.7e-12 / 2.6e-10, 0, 0.03. Filtered x, filtered P, S, NIS and smoothed x are inside the strict tolerance in every scenario (filtered P at most 0.01 of it, smoothed x at most 0.06, NIS at most 2.3e-13 absolute).
  2. The same error appears with IDENTICAL inputs: feeding the NumPy filter output to both smoothers gives a maximum absolute smoothed-P difference of 9.1e-9 (24 violations of the strict tolerance) against 1.1e-8 (18 violations) with each implementation's own filter output; the filter is not the source.
  3. The NumPy reference is itself this sensitive: perturbing the covariances it smooths by 1e-16 relative changes ITS OWN smoothed covariances by up to 1.5e-8 absolute (22 violations), and by 1e-15 relative up to 1.7e-8 (42 violations). tests/test_ukf_numba.py asserts that this self-sensitivity stays below the new bound and above the strict one, so the bound is justified by the code and not by one run.
  Under the new bound every scenario above passes; the largest smoothed-P error is 1.5e-8 absolute, at most 0.27 of the new tolerance in the worst scenario.
- Real data loaded at the time? no; synthetic data only.
- Could this affect G0 or a C1-C4 verdict? How? No: the smoothed covariances are not used by any verdict. Training targets use the smoothed MEANS (strict), the recording-level parameters (strict) and the windows (strict). If a later stage uses smoothed covariances (for example a §9.3 contraction figure, which uses posterior SDs), it must not rely on agreement beyond about 1e-6 relative between the two backends.
- Approved by: Daniel Voldman
- Commit: b93d488
